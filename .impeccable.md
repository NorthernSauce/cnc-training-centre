# Impeccable — CNC Training Centre

Canonical Design Context for this project. See `PRODUCT.md` in the same folder for the full strategic file; this is the impeccable-format mirror used by `/impeccable` commands.

## Design Context

### Users
Two faces of one buyer.

- **Buyer A — engineering company decision-maker** (MD / production manager / engineering manager, SMB engineering, 5–50 staff, CNC machines worth £50k–£500k underperforming because staff lack skills). Reads on desk + phone. Pragmatic, mildly guilty, allergic to marketing fluff. Wants: "Does he know my machine? Roughly what's it cost? Can I trust him?"
- **Buyer B — individual wanting a CNC career** (20s–40s, stuck at operator level or breaking in from a related trade). Reads on phone, evening. Careful with money, wary of sales pitches. Wants: "Will this course get me a job? When is it? How much?"

Both are technical, suspicious of marketing copy, trust people who clearly know their craft. Both will read long articles. Neither tolerates stock-photo-of-engineer-with-laptop heroes.

### Brand Personality
**Plain-spoken, tooled-up, quietly-premium.**

- *Plain-spoken*: David's own voice. Contractions. Short sentences. Swears on Hub articles where it lands; tighter on service pages.
- *Tooled-up*: every paragraph names actual machines, controls, problems. Specificity is the authority.
- *Quietly-premium*: design carries the premium signal; copy never says "premium". Generous space, narrow palette, restrained motion. Premium without "premium".

Voice samples (gold standard, from David's existing articles):
> "I get really pissed off now if I google something to do with CNC machines and I don't find my own article."
> "I fuckin want one." (on a NOMURA sliding head)
> "Get one, just call me in and we'll have some fun together getting it up and running."

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: light. No dark mode at launch. Generous whitespace.
- **Visual register**: industrial-premium. The cleanly-run engineering workshop, not the SaaS lander. Asymmetric editorial layouts over symmetric card grids. Real CNC-specific photography only — no generic stock.
- **Content density**: must scale to 200+ articles + 332 videos in the Learning Hub without oppressive density on the Hub or sparse-looking service pages.
- **Palette anchor**: existing brand blue stays as the primary. Green reserved for video-content tagging only — never a second brand colour. Tinted neutrals (toward the brand blue), no pure black or white. OKLCH throughout.
- **Type**: two families max. Distinctive display + refined body. **Reject** Inter, Roboto, DM Sans, Plus Jakarta, Fraunces, Newsreader, Lora, Crimson, Playfair, Cormorant, IBM Plex, Space Mono, Space Grotesk, Outfit, Instrument *, Syne. Look further than the reflex set.
- **Theme decision**: light, derived from audience context — engineering buyer reading on desk + phone, often during the working day in well-lit shop offices. Light theme matches the reading context.

### Anti-References
- Corporate SaaS landers (Vercel/Stripe/Linear style) — destroys the "physical, tooled, regional" differentiator.
- OEM training brand pages (Mazak/Fanuc/Haas/Siemens corporate training) — erases the "independent alternative" position.
- The current site's own AI-generated SEO pages (Doosan, Hurco) — literally the register we're rebuilding away from.
- All Impeccable absolute bans (gradient text, side-stripe borders >1px, glassmorphism, neon-on-dark, hero metric template, identical 3-card icon grids, centred-everything, generic stock photography).

### Design Principles
1. **Specificity is the proof.** Name actual machines, controls, problems on every page. Generic = corporate = fail.
2. **Restraint is the premium signal.** Whitespace + narrow palette + restrained motion. Premium without "premium".
3. **Content earns the conversion.** Hub does the qualifying; service pages don't oversell. Cross-link articles → services, not the other way.
4. **Both buyers, one site.** Layouts surface routes for company decision-makers AND individual learners without splitting the site in two.
5. **Designed for the workshop, not the boardroom.** High contrast, large touch targets, scannable on phone. Elevated but functional. WCAG 2.1 AA.
