# Strategy: CNC Training Centre

## 1. Hero profile & through-line

**Hero:** Someone on the wrong side of a CNC skills gap. Two faces of the same person:

1. The engineering company decision-maker (MD, production manager, engineering manager) with CNC machines that aren't producing what they should because staff lack the skills to run them properly.
2. The individual who wants to break into CNC machining or level up from operator to programmer — needs formal training and a certificate to prove competence.

**Through-line:** Both need to close a CNC skills gap. The company needs productive machines and capable staff. The individual needs employable skills and proof of competence. Same gap, same solution, same provider.

**Core want (destination):** Competent CNC operators and programmers — either on your shop floor or as yourself. Machines running properly, parts coming off right, skills that pay.

**Core obstacle (barrier):** OEM training is expensive and brand-locked. Free online content is scattered and overwhelming. "We never get round to it." Fear that trained staff will leave. Uncertainty about whether training actually delivers ROI.

**Internal problem:** "We've got the machines. We just don't have anyone who can use them properly — and every month we don't fix that, we're losing money we can't see."

**Voice:** Warm, direct, no-BS. Sweary where it feels natural. Makes people feel at home. Premium quality but anti-corporate. Think: the most experienced bloke in the workshop who also happens to be a brilliant teacher.

**Direct CTA:** Contact David / Enquire (form or phone: 07834 858 407, david@cnctrainingcentre.com)

For engineering companies and individuals who need CNC skills but can't get them from rigid OEM courses or scattered YouTube videos, CNC Training Centre is the independent trainer with 30 years on every major control who teaches around your actual work — not a syllabus — so your machines and your people are productive from day one.

## 7. Learning Hub strategy

**Critical context: The Learning Hub IS the business's marketing engine.**

David generates approximately 10 enquiries per day almost entirely through content — 332 YouTube videos, years of blog articles, LinkedIn posts. His niche is specific enough that anyone searching for CNC training content finds him. The content drives traffic. Traffic drives enquiries. The hub is not a "build trust over 12 months" TAYA play — it's already working. It just needs a proper home.

The Learning Hub on this site is therefore a **content archive and discovery layer** for David's existing (and future) content. Its job:

1. **Organise** — surface 300+ pieces of existing content (articles, videos, guides) in a navigable structure
2. **Attract** — continue driving the niche organic traffic that already works
3. **Qualify** — help the 90% who aren't right self-select out, so the 10% who are right come through warmer and more informed
4. **Convert** — every piece of content is a path to enquiry for the right person

This is bigger than a standard NS Learning Hub. It needs:
- Video embedding (YouTube) alongside written articles
- Category/tag filtering (by machine type, control, skill level, topic)
- Search functionality across the full content library
- A "Start here" pathway for different audiences (companies vs individuals, beginners vs experienced)
- Clear CTAs on every piece of content, but the content itself does the selling — not the CTA copy

### 7c. How the hub drives the business

David's content already generates ~10 enquiries a day. 90% of those aren't suitable (wrong scope, wrong budget, tyre-kickers, people wanting free advice). The hub's secondary job is qualification — helping the wrong people filter themselves out before they pick up the phone, while making the right people more confident to get in touch.

The content does the selling. Someone reads 5 of David's articles or watches 3 of his videos and by the time they enquire, they already know his approach, his pricing ballpark, his personality, and whether he covers their machine. The sales call becomes short and practical: "What machines have you got? When do you want me there?"

Assignment-selling still applies for the 10% who are a good fit:
- When someone asks "what's your day rate?" → send the cost article before the call
- When someone worries their team won't retain what they learn → send "Why CNC training doesn't stick"
- When someone is comparing you to their machine OEM's training → send the OEM vs independent comparison

But the bigger picture: the hub IS the funnel. Content → trust → enquiry. The new site just needs to make that content findable, organised, and connected to the right service pages.

### 7d. Hub structure and migration notes

**What exists (100+ articles, monthly since Dec 2021):** Technical how-to articles in David's voice, course detail pages, service pages, and some newer AI-generated SEO pages (Doosan, Hurco — different voice, need rewriting or dropping).

**Secondary axes (filtering, not primary nav):**

**Migration:**
1. All existing articles migrate into the hub under their current brand category
2. YouTube videos embedded alongside relevant written articles or as standalone hub entries
3. AI-generated SEO pages get rewritten in David's voice or merged into service/training pages
4. Course detail pages become structured sub-pages under Classroom Courses (not hub content)

David keeps doing what he's doing. The hub gives it a proper home with real discoverability instead of a flat WordPress blog archive.
