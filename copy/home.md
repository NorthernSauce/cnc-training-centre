## Agitation

<!-- H2: Bucket A Problem hook. VOC: strategy §3 internal problem verbatim. -->
### I know we should have sorted this ages ago.

<!-- Para 1: VOC swipe from strategy §6 BrandScript "Avoiding failure" verbatim + §3 internal opening line. -->
Every week those machines run slow or sit idle is money you're quietly losing. Work goes out the door that should be done in-house. Staff stay stuck at operator level. Programming gets bottlenecked through one person who learned it fifteen years ago. New starters take months to become useful because nobody has time to train them properly. "We'll sort it next quarter" becomes next year, becomes never.

<!-- Para 2: VOC from strategy §5 narrative para 2 verbatim, em dash killed. -->
You've thought about training before. The OEMs will do it. They charge double what you'd expect, locked to their brand only, following a rigid syllabus that doesn't match your actual jobs. Or there's the internet route: hours of free content, no structure, no one to ask when something doesn't make sense. Neither option fits. So it stays on the list. Another quarter goes by. The machines keep running slow.

<!-- Para 3: VOC from strategy §3 internal problem back half. Closing line echoes §5 narrative. -->
Meanwhile, training feels like a hassle. It's expensive. You're not sure it'll actually stick. Every quarter, the same gap.

## How David trains

**Eyebrow:** How it works

<!-- H2: Formula B from Ch. 10 (curiosity gap). Captures in-action concept without naming it. -->
### What's the only training that doesn't stop the line?

<!-- Para 1: Insight derived from strategy §5 narrative. Lower VOC density on this para - flagged. -->
Most training pulls your team off the floor, sits them in a room with a PowerPoint, hands them a folder, sends them back two days later. The kit didn't run all week. The customer's still waiting. Half of what they learned won't transfer because they never touched the machine.

<!-- Para 2: VOC from strategy §5 narrative para 3 verbatim + §8 phrases verbatim. First-person conversion. -->
I do it the other way round. I come to your shop. I train on your actual machines, with your actual parts. Your operator learns the cycle they're going to run on Monday. The setter watches offsets get dialled in on the part that's due Thursday. By the time I leave, the job is in spec and the person who couldn't do it can.

<!-- Para 3: VOC from strategy §5 narrative verbatim. First-person conversion. "No fixed agenda" verbatim from §8. -->
I assess as I go. If your operators pick something up fast, I move on. If they're struggling, I stay until it clicks. No fixed agenda. Every shop floor is a different fixed agenda already. Yours.
