## What's broken about online CNC training?

<!-- H2: Formula B from Ch. 10 (curiosity gap). Sets up the desire-obstacle frame per DOS. Distinct from all other Hs. -->

<!-- Body para 1: VOC essence from strategy §5 narrative para 2 ("Free online content is bottomless and unsorted"). -->
Free YouTube content gets you partway. Then you hit a question, can't ask anyone, and the video moves on to something else. Free content is bottomless and unsorted, and there's nobody to ask when the video doesn't match the post-processor in front of you.

<!-- Body para 2: Agent-paraphrased (limited VOC for this specific point in strategy). Flagged. -->
Paid online courses exist. Most are 90% theory. Hours of slides on what G-code is, before any code goes on a real control. You sit through a week of foundations and still can't program a part on a real machine.

<!-- Body para 3: The positioning. Echoes "no fixed agenda" + "Your machines, your parts, your pace" essence from §8. -->
So I'm building the online version of the classroom. Same teaching approach, same real-control work, broken into pieces you can do in an evening or a weekend.

## Three things this won't be.

<!-- H2: Bucket E setup. Anti-positioning. Distinct from all other Hs. -->

<!-- Bullets. Anti-positioning per Beat 33 essence (kill the status quo). -->

- **Not 80% theory before any code.** First module gets you setting tools on a real control. Theory comes alongside, not in front.
- **Not a certificate mill.** The certificate evidences what you actually did. Practical work to complete, not just videos to click through.
- **Not endless modules padded out to make the course look longer.** When the course is done, the course is done. No upsells, no "premium tier", no Pro plan.
