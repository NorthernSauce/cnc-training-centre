**01. Send the drawing and tell me what you're running it on.**
Email or send through the enquiry form. Include the drawing (STEP, IGES, DXF, PDF, whatever you've got), the machine and control, the material, the batch quantity, the deadline. The more honest the brief, the cleaner the quote.

**02. I quote on hours, you decide.**
Programming is £60 to £100 per hour plus VAT, depending on complexity. Straightforward 3-axis milling work is at the bottom end. Multi-axis or sliding head with live tooling is at the top. I'll come back with an estimate in hours and a fixed-cap if you'd prefer the certainty. Sub-contract programming is paid upfront before the work starts.

**03. Tested G-code delivered. Phone stays on.**
You get the programme with the post applied, a tooling list, and notes on how it should run. If something kicks back when you load it on the machine (wrong post, fixture clash, anything) get on the phone. I'd rather sort it in twenty minutes with you on the line than have you fighting it for an afternoon.

## If you're building internal capability, this is the wrong service.

<!-- H2: Formula A direct routing-away. BOB principle (Ch. 8). Per Beat 33 essence. Distinct from Onsite + Classroom disqualifier Hs. -->

<!-- Body: existing disqualifier bullets, tightened, em dashes killed. -->

- **You want to build internal capability.** If the reason you're sub-contracting programming is that nobody in-house can do it, and the work isn't going away, training your people is a better long-term answer than paying me by the hour forever. Same hands, different service. Start with the onsite or classroom page.
- **You haven't decided what machine to run it on.** Programming needs the post. If the machine is still being chosen, the programme can't be finalised. I can help with the machine decision as part of a turnkey engagement, but a flat sub-contract job needs the destination locked.
- **You want the cheapest hourly rate available.** I'm not the cheapest sub-contract programmer in the UK. I'm the one who's been on every major control for thirty years and stays on the phone when the programme hits the machine. If hourly rate is the only criterion, you'll find cheaper. Be honest about whether that's actually the right metric for the job.

## What gets asked when someone sends me a drawing?

<!-- H2: Formula B from Ch. 10 (curiosity gap). Distinct from Onsite + Classroom FAQ Hs. -->

**How fast can you turn a programme around?**
Depends on the job. A standard 3-axis milling part with a clean drawing, often same-day if it lands in office hours. A complex multi-axis or sliding head part with live tooling, typically two to five working days. I'd rather quote honestly on the brief than promise a turnaround I can't keep.

**Will the G-code run on my machine without modification?**
That's the goal. I post to your specific machine and control, and the code is tested in Edgecam before it leaves me. Will every programme run perfectly first time on every machine? No. Fixtures, tool wear, controller quirks all exist. That's why the phone stays on after delivery.

**What files do you need?**
Ideally a 3D model (STEP or IGES) plus a PDF drawing for dimensions and tolerances. DXF for 2D work. If all you've got is a hand sketch and a phone call, that can still work for simple parts. I'll tell you on the enquiry whether the brief is enough or whether I need more.

**Do you work with Edgecam only, or can you hand-code?**
Both. Edgecam is the default for anything substantial. Hand-coding still has its place for short, simple programmes or for legacy controls without modern post support. I'll use whichever makes sense for the job and tell you why on the quote.

**What if my batch is too small to justify the programming cost?**
Then say so on the enquiry. Sometimes the right answer is "this part isn't worth programming. You should sub-contract the actual machining out." I'd rather give you that answer than charge you for a programme that doesn't pay back.

**Can you stay involved if the job runs into problems?**
Yes. Phone support is included once the programme has been delivered. If you want me onsite to commission a new machine or train the team running the parts, that's a separate engagement. Onsite training or a turnkey scope.

**Do you do production runs as well as programming?**
Through the CNC machining services route, yes. I work with a network of trusted machine shops. Quoted per job rather than per hour, because the variables shift.
