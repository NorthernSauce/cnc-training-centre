The problem

What's broken about online CNC training?

Free YouTube content gets you partway. Then you hit a question, can't ask anyone, and the video moves on to something else. Free content is bottomless and unsorted, and there's nobody to ask when the video doesn't match the post-processor in front of you.

Paid online courses exist. Most are 90% theory. Hours of slides on what G-code is, before any code goes on a real control. You sit through a week of foundations and still can't program a part on a real machine.

So I'm building the online version of the classroom. Same teaching approach, same real-control work, broken into pieces you can do in an evening or a weekend.

Who it's for

If you can't get to Barwell, this is what's coming.

You're working full-time. Maybe with a young family. Maybe in a part of the country that's a four-hour drive to Leicestershire. Maybe you don't have an employer who'll pay you to take three days off for a classroom course. You can learn this. You just can't learn it in a classroom right now.

Or you're working CNC during the day and want to push into programming on your own time. Evenings. Weekends. Half-hour blocks at lunch. The classroom course is two or three days in one block. The online version is the same content, broken up.

Anti-positioning

Three things this won't be.

  1. A

    Not 80% theory before any code.

    First module gets you setting tools on a real control. Theory comes alongside, not in front.

  2. B

    Not a certificate mill.

    The certificate evidences what you actually did. Practical work to complete, not just videos to click through.

  3. C

    Not endless modules padded out to make the course look longer.

    When the course is done, the course is done. No upsells, no "premium tier", no Pro plan.
