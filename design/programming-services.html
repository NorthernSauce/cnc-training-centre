§ 02 — Who this is for

You'd rather hand the programming over than learn it this week.

You're an engineering company with the work on the books and the machine on the floor. What you haven't got is the time, the headcount, or the in-house programming horsepower to turn the drawing into tested G-code by Friday. Maybe your one programmer is on holiday. Maybe the part is complex enough that hand-coding it would tie someone up for two days. Maybe you've never had a programmer and you've always sub-contracted the trickier work out.

This is the service for that. Not training, not consultancy — programming. I get the drawing, I write the programme, I send it back with the post applied, the tooling listed, and notes on how it should run. You put it on the machine and make parts.

If what you actually need is to build the in-house programming capability so this doesn't keep happening, that's onsite or classroom training. I'd rather steer you to the right thing than book you for the wrong one.

Three steps. Drawing in, G-code out.

§ 06 — Who shouldn't book this

Be honest before you send the drawing.

Sub-contract programming is the right call when you've got a defined job, a deadline, and a need to get parts off the machine. It's the wrong call in a few situations.

  1. 01

    You want to build internal capability.

    If the reason you're sub-contracting programming is that nobody in-house can do it, and the work isn't going away, training your people is a better long-term answer than paying me by the hour forever. Same hands, different service — start with the onsite or classroom page.

  2. 02

    You haven't decided what machine to run it on.

    Programming needs the post. If the machine is still being chosen, the programme can't be finalised. I can help with the machine decision as part of a turnkey engagement, but a flat sub-contract job needs the destination locked.

  3. 03

    You want the cheapest hourly rate available.

    I'm not the cheapest sub-contract programmer in the UK. I'm the one who's been on every major control for thirty years and stays on the phone when the programme hits the machine. If hourly rate is the only criterion, you'll find cheaper. Be honest about whether that's actually the right metric for the job.

§ 09 — FAQ

The questions I get on programming enquiries.

01 How fast can you turn a programme around?

Depends on the job. A standard 3-axis milling part with a clean drawing — often same-day if it lands in office hours. A complex multi-axis or sliding head part with live tooling — typically two to five working days. I'd rather quote honestly on the brief than promise a turnaround I can't keep.

02 Will the G-code run on my machine without modification?

That's the goal. I post to your specific machine and control, and the code is tested in Edgecam before it leaves me. Will every programme run perfectly first time on every machine? No — fixtures, tool wear, controller quirks all exist. That's why the phone stays on after delivery.

03 What files do you need?

Ideally a 3D model (STEP or IGES) plus a PDF drawing for dimensions and tolerances. DXF for 2D work. If all you've got is a hand sketch and a phone call, that can still work for simple parts — I'll tell you on the enquiry whether the brief is enough or whether I need more.

04 Do you work with Edgecam only, or can you hand-code?

Both. Edgecam is the default for anything substantial. Hand-coding still has its place for short, simple programmes or for legacy controls without modern post support. I'll use whichever makes sense for the job and tell you why on the quote.

05 What if my batch is too small to justify the programming cost?

Then say so on the enquiry. Sometimes the right answer is "this part isn't worth programming — you should sub-contract the actual machining out." I'd rather give you that answer than charge you for a programme that doesn't pay back.

06 Can you stay involved if the job runs into problems?

Yes. Phone support is included once the programme has been delivered. If you want me onsite to commission a new machine or train the team running the parts, that's a separate engagement — onsite training or a turnkey scope.

07 Do you do production runs as well as programming?

Through the CNC machining services route, yes — I work with a network of trusted machine shops. Quoted per job rather than per hour, because the variables shift.
