§ 02 — Who this is for

You run the workshop.
The machines aren't doing what they should.

You're an MD, a production manager, an engineering manager. You've got six-figure CNC machines on the floor and the people running them are working off muscle memory and best guesses. The fast jobs take a day. The complicated jobs go out to a subcontractor because nobody in-house can program them. One person knows the Mazak properly and you wince every time he books holiday.

That's the gap I close. I come in, work with the team you've got, on the kit you've got, and leave with everyone two or three rungs further up the ladder than they were on Monday. Not in a classroom on a simulator. On your machine, with your fixtures, programming your parts.

§ 03 — What's included

A working week on your floor, not a slide deck.

Every onsite engagement is built around your situation, but here's what comes as standard.

07 items · standard scope

    Typically 2 to 5 days, scoped on the enquiry call. Longer engagements split across visits if it suits the production schedule.

  2. 02

    Whatever's on the floor: mills, lathes, sliding head, multi-axis, single control or a mixed shop with five different brands.

    Fanuc, Heidenhain, Siemens, Mazak/Mazatrol, Hurco, Brother, Haas, Doosan, XYZ, sliding head. If you've got a control I haven't named, ask — the answer is probably yes.

    No fixed syllabus. If your operators pick something up in twenty minutes, I move on. If they're struggling, I stay on it until it clicks. They go home knowing it, not having seen a slide about it.

    For each person trained, so it's evidenced for your customers, your auditors, and the operator's own CV.

  7. 07

    Once I'm off site, the phone stays on. If something kicks back on the next part they program, I'd rather get the call than have it sit broken for a week.

Three steps. No drawn-out sales process.

§ 06 — Who shouldn't book this

Be honest with yourself before you pick up the phone.

Onsite training is the right call when you've got machines on the floor, a team that needs lifting, and the work to apply the training to once I'm gone. It's the wrong call in a few situations and I'd rather say so up front than take the booking.

  1. A

    You haven't got the machines yet.

    If the kit is on order or you're still scoping a purchase, classroom training on simulators is the better starting point. Onsite makes sense once the machines are in.

  2. B

    You want one person trained to expert level in two days.

    That's not how skills work. I can move someone meaningfully along the ladder in a week. Mastery takes the months after I leave.

  3. C

    You're looking for the cheapest quote in the market.

    The OEMs often charge double my day rate, but I'm not the cheapest independent option either. I'm the one who's been doing this for thirty years on every control you can name. Price-shop if that's the priority — just be honest about it.

  4. D

    You want a fixed syllabus to tick a compliance box.

    I don't work to agenda. If your situation needs a rigid, accredited course structure, the OEM route fits better.

If any of those land, say so on the enquiry call. I'll route you somewhere that fits, including a competitor if that's the honest answer.

§ 09 — FAQ

The questions I get on every enquiry call.

01 Do you know my machine?

Probably. I've trained on every major control — Fanuc, Heidenhain, Siemens, Mazak, Hurco, Brother, Haas, Doosan, XYZ, sliding head — and most of the common machine builders. Tell me what you've got on the floor and I'll be straight with you about whether it's somewhere I can add value or somewhere you'd be better off with the OEM.

02 Is your day rate per person or for the whole team?

Whole team. £700/day covers two operators or six. Per-person pricing on onsite training is one of the reasons OEM quotes come in so high — if you've got four people who need bringing up, that's four times the bill. Here it's a flat rate.

03 How long are you onsite?

Depends on the scope. Two days for a focused engagement (e.g. all your operators up to confident programming of standard parts on the Fanuc). A full week if you're lifting a team from operator level to programming on multi-axis. I'd rather scope it honestly on the enquiry call than oversell the engagement.

04 Will my team get certificates?

Yes, one per person trained. Evidenced for your customers, your auditors, and the operator's own record.

05 What if my operators learn faster or slower than expected?

That's the whole point of adapting as I go. If they're flying, I push further. If they're struggling, I stay on it until it sticks. The day rate is fixed; the scope flexes to fit the people in front of me.

06 What happens if my team forgets it the moment I leave?

Two answers. First, training on your actual parts, on your actual machine, means the skills get applied the day after I leave — that's the strongest retention there is. Second, the phone stays on. If something kicks back in the weeks after, get in touch.

07 Do you work nationwide?

Yes. I'm based in Barwell, Leicestershire and travel UK-wide. If you're a long way north or south, I'll flag the travel impact on the quote rather than hide it.
